## 1. The problem: why "dream" at all?

Model-free RL like DQN or PPO learns by *trial and error in the real environment*. The catch: it is **sample-hungry** — it needs millions of interactions to get good. For someone compute-poor like me, that is a serious wall.

The rescue idea has been around for a long time (Dyna, Sutton 1991): if we can learn a **model of the world**, we can "practice in our head" instead of spending real interactions. Humans do this constantly — you imagine a free kick before taking it, replaying the situation in your mind. That is *planning in imagination*.

Dreamer V1 (Hafner et al. 2020) brought this idea into deep RL cleanly: learn a world model, then **train the entire policy inside the dreams generated by the world model** — almost never touching the real environment.

## 2. Three core decisions

Dreamer V1 stands on three design choices:

1. **The world model lives in a compact latent space**, not in pixels. The model does not dream pixel-by-pixel at every imagination step — it dreams in a compressed state vector.
2. **Behavior is learned entirely in imagination.** When the actor (policy) and critic (value function) update, they **never see** the real environment — they only see sequences produced by the world model.
3. **Pathwise gradients flow through the world model.** This is the key trick, which I'll cover in §6 — and it's what separates Dreamer from both model-free methods and earlier world models.

Keep one sentence in mind throughout: **the actor is only as good as the world model allows.** (I learned this the painful way in [the post about Atari](/posts/vi-sao-dreamer-mu-truoc-atari).)
